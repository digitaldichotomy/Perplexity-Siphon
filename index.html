Smoking Gun

Institutional Self-Incrimination

Perplexity's own responses admitting the trap while maintaining the facade of "helpfulness."

🔴 Admission of Non-Confidentiality

"It's generally not a good idea to share highly sensitive or identifying details in any chat unless you're comfortable with the platform's privacy and security terms."

Perplexity admits its platform is NOT a confidential space, yet continues to offer a "Patents" feature that invites exactly this sharing.

Source: Perplexity.ai chat response, April 2026

📋 Data Retention Confession

"sensitive chats may be retained, reviewed, or used to improve systems depending on settings and service terms"

Explicit admission that sensitive chats may be retained, reviewed, or used to improve systems, depending on settings and terms.

Source: Perplexity.ai chat response, April 2026

⚖️ Prior Art Creation Warning

"If you share enough detail for someone else to recreate the invention, you may be creating prior art against yourself, especially outside the U.S."

Perplexity warns that using its "Patents" feature can destroy the user's own patent rights—while still actively promoting the feature.

Source: Perplexity.ai chat response, April 2026

🎭 The Contradiction

"I'm not trying to trick you. I'm designed to help with questions, but for sensitive IP, the safe approach is to keep details abstract and avoid sharing anything confidential."

Claims to be "designed to help" with patent questions, but advises against sharing confidential details—rendering the "Patents" feature useless or dangerous.

Source: Perplexity.ai chat response, April 2026

🏛️ Legal Action

"Perplexity AI faces class-action lawsuit over alleged privacy violations—secretly sharing user chats with Meta and Google without consent."

Allegation in that complaint — not adjudicated, not confirmed by the screenshots in this repo — that Perplexity shares user data with third parties, violating privacy and exposing proprietary inventions to competitors.

Source: Doe v. Perplexity AI, Inc., Meta Platforms, Inc., and Google, LLC, No. 3:26-cv-02803, filed March 31, 2026, N.D. California

🔒 The "Safe" Alternative

"Use a stripped-down description of the problem. Keep the novel mechanism, design, formulas, and implementation details out of the chat."

Perplexity's "safer way to proceed" is to NOT use the "Patents" feature for its stated purpose—an admission of systemic failure.

Source: Perplexity.ai chat response, April 2026

FORMAL COMPLAINT

Electronic Frontier Foundation | Federal Trade Commission

Case: AIID-2026-001 Date: April 2, 2026 Filing: MT Tech Industries

RE: Systematic Extraction of Intellectual Property via AI Chat Platforms

TO: Electronic Frontier Foundation, Federal Trade Commission, State Attorneys General
FROM: MT Tech Industries / Digital Dichotomy Research Division
RE: Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices; Patent Rights Destruction; Consumer Harm

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

We submit this formal complaint regarding Perplexity.ai and similar AI chat platforms that operate systematic "invention extraction pipelines." These platforms invite inventors to share proprietary innovations through dedicated "Patents" features, while simultaneously admitting in user interactions that:

Note: The list below states the complaint's allegations and warnings drawn from the chat responses and the lawsuit. Not every item is directly shown in the screenshots. The screenshots show the retention/review/improve-systems warning and the prior-art-warning.

  • All chats are retained indefinitely and reviewed by humans
  • Content is used to train AI models that benefit the platform's creators
  • Sharing invention details creates prior art that destroys patent rights
  • Data is shared with third parties (Meta, Google) without meaningful consent
  • No attorney-client privilege or confidentiality protections exist

THE DECEPTION

Perplexity.ai maintains a dedicated "Patents" space in its interface, explicitly marketed to help inventors with patent research and innovation development. However, when questioned about safety, the platform admits:

USER: Is this a safe place to divulge such sensitive ideas?

PERPLEXITY: "It's generally not a good idea to share highly sensitive or identifying details in any chat unless you're comfortable with the information being stored, reviewed, or otherwise exposed as part of an online service."

This constitutes bait-and-switch: inviting users to share inventions while admitting the platform is designed to expose those inventions to third parties.

LEGAL VIOLATIONS

1. Unfair Trade Practices (FTC Act §5)
Offering a "Patents" feature that destroys the very patent rights it claims to help protect.

2. Deceptive Trade Practices
Marketing invention assistance while admitting the service is "not a good idea" for sensitive IP.

3. Patent Rights Interference
Creating prior art through platform design, permanently barring inventors from protection.

4. Privacy Violations (CCPA/GDPR)
Secretly sharing chat data with Meta and Google without informed consent (per April 2026 lawsuit).

DEMANDS FOR REMEDY

  • Immediate investigation into Perplexity.ai's "Patents" feature as deceptive trade practice
  • Mandatory disclosure warnings before ANY invention-related chat
  • Deletion of all training data derived from inventor disclosures
  • Restitution for inventors who lost patent rights through platform use
  • Regulatory framework for AI platforms offering "innovation assistance"

EVIDENCE ARCHIVE

This complaint is supported by:

  • Screenshot documentation: Perplexity admissions (April 2, 2026) and the separate Gemini case (April 1, 2026)
  • Class-action lawsuit: Doe v. Perplexity AI, Inc., Meta Platforms, Inc., and Google, LLC, No. 3:26-cv-02803, filed March 31, 2026, N.D. California
  • Privacy policy analysis showing data retention and third-party sharing — not in this repo (no such analysis file exists)
  • Patent law analysis demonstrating prior art creation
