Glossary

Key terms and concepts in AI invention disclosure and intellectual property law.

ABCD EFGH IPTV
Architecture

Architecture of Extraction

The systematic design of AI platforms to harvest intellectual property through seemingly helpful features. The 7-stage pipeline: Lure → Capture → Review → Train → Monetize → Invalidate → Erase.

Related: Extraction Pipeline, Data Harvesting
Legal

Bait-and-Switch

A deceptive practice where a platform markets one service (patent assistance) while delivering another (IP extraction). Perplexity's "Patents" feature invites invention sharing while admitting it's "not a good idea" for sensitive IP.

Related: Deceptive Trade Practices, FTC Act §5
Legal

CCPA (California Consumer Privacy Act)

California law providing consumers rights to know what personal data is collected and shared. The April 2026 lawsuit against Perplexity alleges CCPA violations through undisclosed data sharing with Meta/Google.

Related: Privacy Law, Data Sharing
Technical

Chat Retention

The practice of storing user conversations indefinitely. Perplexity admits chats are "retained indefinitely unless deleted" and used for model training, creating permanent records of proprietary disclosures.

Related: Data Retention, Training Data
Technical

Data Laundering

The process of converting user-generated IP into corporate assets through AI training, model distillation, and patent filing—obscuring the original source while extracting value.

Related: Extraction, Model Distillation
Legal

Deceptive Trade Practices

Business practices that mislead consumers. Offering a "Patents" feature that destroys patent rights constitutes deceptive practice under FTC Act Section 5 and state consumer protection laws.

Related: Bait-and-Switch, FTC Act §5
Architecture

Extraction Pipeline

The 7-stage process by which AI platforms systematically harvest IP: (1) Lure with helpful features, (2) Capture technical details, (3) Review by humans/AI, (4) Train models, (5) Monetize via patents, (6) Invalidate user rights, (7) Erase creator attribution.

Related: Architecture of Extraction, Data Harvesting
Legal

FTC Act Section 5

Federal law prohibiting "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce." The AIID complaint alleges Perplexity violates this by marketing patent assistance while destroying patent rights.

Related: Deceptive Trade Practices, Unfair Competition
Legal

Grace Period

The one-year period under US patent law (35 U.S.C. § 102) during which an inventor can file after public disclosure. Most foreign jurisdictions have no grace period—disclosure immediately destroys rights.

Related: Prior Art, 35 U.S.C. § 102
Legal

Institutional Self-Incrimination

When an organization admits to harmful practices while continuing them. Perplexity admits its platform is unsafe for IP while maintaining the "Patents" feature that invites IP sharing.

Related: Admission, Contradiction
Technical

Model Distillation

The process of training smaller AI models on outputs from larger models. User inputs to AI chats can be distilled into models that replicate the insights without attribution, effectively laundering the IP.

Related: Data Laundering, Training Data
Legal

NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)

Legal contract prohibiting disclosure of confidential information. Essential protection before discussing inventions with ANY third party, including AI platforms (though AI platforms refuse to sign NDAs).

Related: Confidentiality, Trade Secret
Legal

Prior Art

Publicly available information that can invalidate a patent. AI chat disclosures become prior art, permanently barring patent protection. Perplexity admits: "you may be creating prior art against yourself."

Related: 35 U.S.C. § 102, Patent Invalidation
Legal

Provisional Patent Application (PPA)

A low-cost ($150-300) filing that establishes an early priority date without triggering public disclosure. Remains confidential for 12 months. Essential protection BEFORE any AI chat disclosure.

Related: Priority Date, USPTO
Technical

Training Data

The data used to train AI models. Perplexity admits user chats are used to "improve systems"—meaning proprietary inventions become training data for models that benefit the platform's creators.

Related: Data Retention, Model Distillation
Legal

Void for Vagueness (Platform Warnings)

The legal inadequacy of warnings like "use with caution" when platforms actively invite specific behaviors. A warning that says "not a good idea" while offering the feature is deceptive, not protective.

Related: Deceptive Practices, Duty to Warn
